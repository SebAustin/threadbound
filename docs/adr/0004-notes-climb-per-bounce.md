# A thread's notes climb per bounce

Pitch used to come from a thread's length alone, so 17 of the 24 solutions played one repeated note, which undercut "every solution is a melody". Now a thread's length sets only its root. Each further bounce on that thread during a drop plays the next note of its climb: up the pentatonic scale from the root to the top, then down from just below the root to the bottom, then from the root again. That way a thread sounds all ten notes before any repeats. All of a drop's marbles share the climb, and it restarts at the root on every drop. A solve closes on a cadence from the cups. Notes are decoupled from physics, so restitution, the level proofs and "shorter rings higher" stay as they were.

**Considered Options:** a plain mirror that turns at the top and walks back down (w1-03's root is G5, one step below the top, so three single bounces play G5 A5 G5: only two notes); widening the length-to-pitch range (it can't vary a single-thread level); a "fretboard" where the landing point picks the note (it ties music to landing positions a few millimetres apart); fixing it only in the video (dishonest).

**Consequences:** the same solution always plays the same tune, so the melody book stays meaningful, and its entries now keep their timing. Melody capture still samples once per rendered frame, so scoring a melody (a "song star") stays out until capture is proven frame-rate independent.
